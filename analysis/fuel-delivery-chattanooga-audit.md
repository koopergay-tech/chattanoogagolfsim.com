# Residential At-Home Fuel Delivery — Greater Chattanooga Business Audit

**Prepared:** August 29, 2026
**Scope:** Should you start a residential (at-home) fuel delivery business in the greater Chattanooga area? If yes, what does it cost and what's the best path? If no, what's the nearest viable alternative?

---

## Executive Summary — The Verdict

### ❌ NO-GO: Residential gasoline delivery to driveways, as conceived.

### ✅ CONDITIONAL GO: A diesel-first, B2B mobile fueling business (job sites, small fleets, generators, marinas) with a lean ~$80K–$130K entry — *only if* you pre-sell anchor accounts before buying equipment.

The residential model fails on four independent tests, any one of which would be disqualifying:

1. **The national track record is 0-for-6.** Every funded US residential gas-delivery startup — Booster ($236M+ raised), Yoshi, Filld, WeFuel, Purple — is dead or pivoted to fleet fueling. Yoshi sold its *entire fuel division* for just $2.0M in late 2024. The only public operator (EzFill/NextNRG) runs at 7–8% gross margin (~$0.27–0.33 gross profit per gallon), has **never been profitable**, and grows almost entirely on fleet gallons.
2. **The math doesn't clear the driver's wage.** A 12-gallon car fill at a ~$0.30/gal margin plus a $5 fee grosses under $10 per stop, against a fuel-truck driver costing $25–32/hour plus truck, insurance, and card fees — you need 4–6 stops *per hour, sustained*, in a metro that sprawls at ~1,426 people/sq mi across ridges and river crossings.
3. **Chattanooga is close to the worst US market for the value proposition.** Tennessee is the 3rd-cheapest gas state; Chattanooga is repeatedly the cheapest metro in Tennessee (~$3.44/gal vs ~$4.10 national in Aug 2026), with ~266 gas stations and a 24.5-minute mean commute. Customers save nothing and are never far from a pump.
4. **A local player already exists, and the law is against the gasoline version.** Topped Off (toppedoff.co) already sells $35/month "AutoFill" gasoline delivery across the exact target suburbs. Meanwhile, fire code (IFC §5707 / NFPA 30A ch. 14) makes residential gasoline dispensing discretionary with the Fire Marshal and requires a fueling point ≥25 ft from buildings and lot lines — which most driveways physically cannot provide — and **Georgia prohibits on-demand retail mobile fueling entirely**, striking the Catoosa/Walker county side of the metro.

Where fuel delivery *does* make money is instructive: propane and heating-oil companies drop 100–300 gallons into a **stationary home tank** at $0.85–$2.00/gal margin — **$85–$600 gross per stop**, or 30–60x the residential gasoline stop. Fleet "wet hosing" pumps hundreds of gallons into clustered vehicles overnight on contract. The gallons and margins are in diesel and B2B — and in Chattanooga ("Freight Alley": Covenant, US Xpress, Kenco; a 46,000-unit housing construction pipeline; marina clusters on Chickamauga Lake) that demand is deep, though contested by incumbent jobbers (JAT Energy, Hart Fueling, Fuel Logic).

**Bottom line:** Don't copy Topped Off. If you want into fuel, enter through the diesel/B2B door with pre-sold accounts, or don't enter at all.

## 2. National Evidence: The Competitor Graveyard

| Company | Raised | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Booster Fuels (2015) | $236M+ | Abandoned consumer in 2020; now 100% fleet (Amazon, UPS, FedEx, Walmart) |
| Yoshi (2015, YC) | GM/Bridgestone-backed | Pivoted to fleet; **sold entire fuel division to EzFill for $2.0M (Dec 2024)** |

| Purple (2015) | Uber co-founder backed | Closed |
| EzFill → NextNRG (NASDAQ: NXXT) | Public | Alive — but fleet-driven and never profitable (see below) |

**EzFill/NextNRG is the industry's only public financial disclosure, and it's damning for the residential thesis:**

| Year | Revenue | Gross margin | Net loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $23.2M | 6% | −$10.5M |
| 2024 | $27.8M | 8% | −$16.2M |
| 2025 | $81.8M (acquisition-driven) | 8.4% | −$88.2M (−$7.1M adj. EBITDA) |
| Q2 2026 | $27.7M | 7.2% | −$6.6M |

Gross profit per gallon: **$0.27 (Q2 2025) → $0.33 (Q2 2026)** — *before* any operating cost. Management attributes growth to commercial fleets; residential is the minority and its charge is only $4.99/delivery or $9.99/month. Historical precedent: SMF Energy, the original public wet-hosing company, went Chapter 11 in 2012.

The one residential success, CAFU (Dubai), operates with cheap labor, extreme density, and state tolerance — not a transferable comp. New US entrants keep trying (Gas Dash in Atlanta, May 2026; Pumped Up Fuel in Charlotte; FuelFox in Birmingham) — note that FuelFox, the nearest structural comp, grew to ~$20M by going **fleet/workplace, not driveways**, and Atlanta is ten times Chattanooga's size.

## 3. Local Market: Greater Chattanooga

**Demographics (favorable on paper):** MSA 594,530 (2025, +5.5% since 2020 — 2x US rate, driven by inbound movers), ~231,000 households, median HH income ~$72–75K. Affluent pockets: Lookout Mountain (~$201K median HH income), Signal Mountain (~$146K), Ooltewah 37363 (~$97K, 39% of households >$100K), East Brainerd 37421 (~$83K). Perhaps 25–40K households >$100K in the plausible service area. Average 2 vehicles/household.

**But the demand fundamentals are hostile:**

- **Gas is cheap and everywhere.** Chattanooga metro ~$3.44/gal (late Aug 2026) vs ~$4.10 national; TN is the 3rd-cheapest state; AAA has repeatedly named Chattanooga the cheapest metro in TN. ~266 stations in/near the metro (RaceTrac, QuikTrip, Circle K, MAPCO...). A delivery markup is a *large percentage* on a $3.44 base with a pump on every corner.
- **Short commutes:** 24.5-min mean (90% of US average). Less tank burn, easy pump access.
- **Sprawl kills route density:** urban-area density ~1,426/sq mi (vs 2,500–4,000+ in metros where routes could work), fragmented by the river, ridges, and mountain roads. Growth is concentrated in the unincorporated fringe — the *worst* geometry for stops-per-hour.
- **EV factor:** modest headwind, recently weakened — VW is ending ID.4 EV production in Chattanooga (April 2026) and retooling for the gas Atlas. ICE dominates the local fleet for the audit horizon. Neutral-to-slightly-positive for fuel demand; does not rescue the economics.

**Competition:**

- **Topped Off (toppedoff.co)** — Chattanooga residential gasoline delivery, already live: $35/mo AutoFill subscription, $15 one-time top-off, fleet discounts; service area = East Brainerd, Apison, Ooltewah, Collegedale, Lookout Mtn, Signal Mtn, Hixson, Red Bank, Soddy Daisy, Middle Valley, Harrison. *Their operating status/scale needs a direct check (site unreachable from this research environment) — but either answer hurts the thesis: if they're thriving, the first-mover has the small niche; if they're struggling, the market has spoken.*
- **Commercial/fleet mobile fueling already in the metro** (who you'd fight in the diesel pivot): JAT Energy (local, since 1976), Hart Fueling, Fuel Logic, Moffitt Services, Brad Hall, Robco Fuels, Great Lakes Petroleum, Rogers Petroleum.
- **Roadside "out of gas" rescue** (skims the emergency niche): DRIVE Roadside, Bell's Towing, Five Star, AAA.
- **Supply-side positive:** Chattanooga has its own terminal rack (Colonial Pipeline, Sunoco LP, Kinder Morgan at Cromwell Rd/Bonny Oaks) — a new entrant can buy local rack fuel like the incumbents do.

**Where the gallons actually are locally:** Freight Alley trucking (Covenant ~2,300 tractors; US Xpress; Kenco; ~42% of regional employment logistics-linked), a 46,000-unit 20-year housing construction pipeline (job-site diesel for equipment/generators), ~137+ landscaping companies, 15+ marinas on Chickamauga Lake/Tennessee River, standby generators on the mountains.

## 4. Regulatory Reality (the quiet killer)

| Question | Gasoline (Class I flammable) | Diesel (Class II combustible) |
|---|---|---|
| Fire code | IFC §5707 / NFPA 30A ch.14: only with Fire Marshal (AHJ) operational permit + approved locations; fueling point ≥25 ft from buildings/lot lines, truck ≥10 ft, banned on public streets & in structures. Most driveways physically fail the 25-ft test. WA-style precedents ban it outright in residential zones; SF/LA/Seattle stopped the startups. | Long-established residential practice (heating-oil model); no residential-zone prohibitions found |
| Georgia side of metro | **Prohibited** — Ga. Comp. R. 120-3-11 bans on-demand retail mobile fueling (verify scope with GA Safety Fire Commissioner) | Restricted similarly for retail on-demand; verify |
| DOT/hazmat | Placards, shipping papers, CDL with hazmat+tanker (X) once >119-gal tanks or ≥1,001 lb aggregate; PHMSA registration $275/yr | **≤119-gal tanks: excepted from hazmat rules entirely** (49 CFR 173.150(f)) — multi-tank trailers haul up to ~920 gal with no hazmat endorsement, often no CDL |
| TN specifics | TN adopted 2021 IFC statewide (eff. Aug 2025); Chattanooga runs 2018 IFC — both contain §5707. TN fuel taxes: gas $0.274/gal, diesel $0.284 (buy tax-paid at rack via jobber → no distributor license burden; likely transporter-class license only — confirm with TN DOR). Truck meters must be TN Dept. of Agriculture W&M certified. | Same, easier |

**Decisive unknown:** the Chattanooga Fire Prevention Bureau's posture on residential gasoline dispensing — pure AHJ discretion, nothing published. This is phone call #1 before spending a dollar (see §8). Topped Off's existence suggests *some* accommodation or sub-threshold pickup setup, but that's an assumption, not a fact.

## 5. Unit Economics: Why Residential Gasoline Loses

**Per-stop math (residential gasoline):**

```
12 gal fill × $0.30/gal margin        =  $3.60
+ delivery fee                        =  $5.00
Gross per stop                        =  $8.60
− card processing (2.9% + $0.30)      ≈ −$1.75
Net contribution per stop             ≈  $6.85

Driver (loaded)                       ≈ $32–40/hr
Truck ops + insurance allocation      ≈ $10–14/hr
Required stops/hour to break even     ≈ 5–7
Achievable stops/hour in this sprawl  ≈ 2.5–3.5
→ Structural loss of ~$20–30 per driver-hour
```

Fixing it requires a $15–20 per-stop fee (Topped Off's one-time price is $15 — telling), which collapses demand in a cheap-gas market. Subscription math is no better: 500 subscribers at $35/mo ≈ $17.5K/mo + ~$7K fuel margin against ~$28–35K/mo of two-truck operating cost — and 500 paying households is likely *years* of marketing in a metro where the pioneers' national conversion rates never got close.

**Per-stop math (B2B diesel, the contrast):**

```
250 gal job-site/fleet drop × $0.40–0.50/gal + drop fee ≈ $110–150 gross per stop
6–8 stops/day, schedulable overnight/clustered routes
Same truck, same driver → 12–20x the revenue per hour
```

**Break-even sketch, lean owner-operated diesel business:** fixed costs ≈ $50–60K/yr (insurance ~$20K, equipment ~$15K, software/licenses/marketing ~$15–20K) before owner pay. At $0.45/gal blended margin, ~275–350K gal/yr (≈1,100–1,400 gal/day) supports ~$100K+ owner income. A realistic year-one ramp (50–120K gal) means the first year is a grind — which is why anchor accounts must be pre-sold, not hoped for.

## 6. What It Would Cost

### Path A — The idea as asked: residential gasoline (NOT recommended)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| 2 sub-119-gal pickup rigs (trucks + DOT transfer tanks + certified meters) | $110–150K |
| Insurance (auto+GL+pollution, 2 vehicles) | $30–50K/yr |
| Drivers (2, CDL not strictly required sub-threshold) | $110–130K/yr loaded |
| Software/app, payments, marketing to reach ~500 subs | $40–80K yr 1 |
| Licenses, permits, W&M, working capital & fuel float | $15–25K |
| **Year-one all-in** | **~$250–400K** — with the expected outcome being the same as Booster/Yoshi/Filld's |

A lean solo copycat (~$75–120K) is possible but competes head-on with Topped Off for a niche nobody in America has made profitable.

### Path B — Recommended: lean diesel-first B2B ("conditional go")

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Used 3/4–1-ton pickup (or existing truck) | $35–55K |
| Thunder Creek–style multi-tank 920-gal diesel trailer (no hazmat/no CDL) | $14–18K |
| W&M-certified meter/pump package | $3–7K |
| Insurance (auto + GL + pollution) | $15–25K/yr |
| Fuel float (1 load) + working capital | $5–10K |
| Software (~$99/truck/mo class), licenses, PHMSA n/a sub-threshold, marketing | $8–15K |
| **All-in year-one entry** | **~$80–130K** (as low as ~$60K with equipment you already own) |
| Scale step (later): used 1,000–3,000-gal fuel truck $25–90K + CDL-X driver ~$60–70K loaded + hazmat insurance ~$19K/yr | only after route density proves out |

### Path C — Do neither

Park the capital. This audit's honest expected value ranks C > B > A unless the §8 pre-sales test passes.

## 8. If You Go: The Right Way (90-day, gate-by-gate)

**Gate 0 — Five phone calls before any money (week 1–2):**
1. **Chattanooga Fire Prevention Bureau** — will they permit on-demand mobile fueling; what vehicle class/locations; anything residential ever approved?
2. **TN DOR Motor Fuel section** — confirm license class (transporter vs wholesaler) + bond for a tax-paid rack-purchase retail model.
3. **TN Dept. of Agriculture W&M** ((615) 837-5109) — meter certification process.
4. **Two insurance brokers** (fuel-hauler specialists) — real quotes, not ranges.
5. **A Chattanooga rack jobber** (e.g., via JAT/Rogers relationships or direct Sunoco/Colonial terminal contacts) — actual rack+freight cost basis.

**Gate 1 — Pre-sell before buying (weeks 2–8):** get **3–5 signed anchor commitments** — e.g., two homebuilders/GCs in the Ooltewah–Apison corridor (equipment + generator diesel), one landscaping fleet (weekly gas-can/equipment program), one marina or dock cluster, one small delivery/trade fleet (5–15 vans, overnight wet-hosing). Target ≥800 committed gal/week. **No commitments → stop; you've spent under $2K to learn the truth.**

**Gate 2 — Minimum viable fleet (weeks 8–12):** buy the trailer rig (Path B), get insured, licensed, metered; run the anchor routes; take residential *diesel* orders opportunistically (generators, tractors, boats — 100-gal minimums) since they're legal and decent per-stop.

**Gate 3 — Density before hardware:** only when routes are full (>1,000 gal/day sustained), add the used bobtail + CDL-X driver, and *then* evaluate whether a premium residential gasoline add-on (Fire Marshal permitting) is worth piloting at a $15–20 true-cost fee — with the explicit expectation it stays a niche upsell, never the core.

**Also do:** a 30-minute diligence pass on Topped Off (order once, talk to customers, check their truck setup and permit posture) — it's the cheapest market research available in this metro.
